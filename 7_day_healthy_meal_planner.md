## Meal Planning Approach

This 7-day plan is designed for a working professional and emphasizes:

- Balanced protein, carbohydrates, healthy fats, vegetables, fruits, and fiber.
- Mostly home-cooked, practical Indian/South Indian foods.
- Simple preparation and meal-prep-friendly ingredients.
- Adequate hydration throughout the day.
- Variety across grains, pulses, vegetables, fruits, nuts, seeds, dairy/alternatives, eggs, and lean non-vegetarian protein.
- Moderate use of oil, salt, sugar, fried foods, and highly processed foods.

**Portion note:** Portions should be adjusted according to age, sex, body size, activity level, goals, and medical requirements. The plan below is a general healthy-food template rather than individualized medical nutrition advice.

## Daily Nutrition Checklist

| Nutrition Area | Practical Daily Target |
|---|---|
| Protein | Include a protein source at every major meal: eggs, fish, chicken, paneer, curd, tofu, soy, dal, chickpeas, rajma, sprouts, etc. |
| Vegetables | Aim for several servings across lunch and dinner, with different colors during the week |
| Fruit | About 1–2 servings of whole fruit daily |
| Whole grains | Prefer oats, millets, brown rice, whole wheat, and other minimally processed grains regularly |
| Healthy fats | Nuts, seeds, peanuts, and moderate amounts of cooking oils |
| Dairy/alternatives | Curd, buttermilk, milk, paneer, or suitable fortified alternatives as tolerated |
| Fiber | Pulses, vegetables, fruits, whole grains, nuts, and seeds |
| Hydration | Drink water regularly throughout the day; increase intake with heat and exercise |
| Added sugar | Keep low; prefer whole fruit over juices and sweetened drinks |
| Fried/ultra-processed foods | Keep occasional rather than daily |

## Working-Professional Meal-Prep Strategy

### Weekend Preparation

Prepare a few components in advance:

1. Wash and chop commonly used vegetables.
2. Cook a batch of dal/chana/rajma and refrigerate or freeze portions.
3. Prepare a basic tomato-onion/vegetable base for quick curries.
4. Boil eggs in advance if included in the diet.
5. Portion nuts and seeds into small containers.
6. Keep curd/buttermilk ingredients readily available.
7. Cook grains in batches where practical.
8. Keep washed fruits available for quick snacks.

### Office-Friendly Snack Options

Choose one when hungry between meals:

- Fruit + nuts
- Roasted chana
- Sprouts salad
- Unsweetened curd + seeds
- Buttermilk
- Boiled eggs
- Homemade paneer/tofu cubes
- Makhana with minimal oil
- Peanut chaat

## Foods to Limit

- Sugary soft drinks and packaged juices
- Frequent sweets and desserts
- Deep-fried snacks
- Processed meats
- Excessively salty packaged foods
- Refined-flour-heavy meals as everyday staples
- Frequent restaurant/fast-food meals
- Excess alcohol, if applicable

## Important Notes

This is a general balanced meal plan for a healthy adult. Exact calorie and protein requirements vary substantially by body weight, activity level, goals, and health status. Anyone with diabetes, kidney disease, food allergies, gastrointestinal disease, or other medical conditions should have the plan individualized by a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian.

For fat loss, the same food structure can generally be used with appropriately reduced portions and adequate protein rather than relying on skipping meals or extreme restriction.
